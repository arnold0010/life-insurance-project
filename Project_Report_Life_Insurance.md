## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Life insurance occupies a unique position in the financial planning of an individual because it combines two important needs — **protection** against the financial consequences of premature death and **savings/investment** for future goals. In a developing economy like India, where social security cover is limited, life insurance plays a crucial role in providing financial security to families.

This project, titled **"A Study on Perception of Investors Investing in Life Insurance,"** attempts to understand how investors view life insurance as an investment and protection instrument, what factors influence their buying decisions, their level of awareness about various products, and their satisfaction with insurers.

The study is based on **primary data** collected from **100 respondents** through a structured questionnaire, supported by **secondary data** from journals, books, IRDAI reports, and company websites. Simple statistical tools such as percentages, tables, and charts have been used for analysis.

The findings reveal that **safety and security of the family** is the most important reason for investing in life insurance, followed by **tax benefits** and **savings**. A large proportion of investors still prefer **LIC** because of trust and the government backing, although awareness of private insurers is rising. The study also notes that many investors have only **moderate awareness** of the different types of policies available and that **agents** remain the most influential source of information.

Based on the analysis, the report offers suggestions to insurers regarding **customer education, transparency in returns, faster claim settlement, and digital service improvement** to enhance investor confidence.

# CHAPTER 1: INTRODUCTION

## 1.1 Introduction to the Study

Investment is the commitment of funds today with the expectation of receiving a return in the future. Every individual earns income and, after meeting current consumption needs, sets aside a part of it for the future. The way in which this surplus is channelled depends largely on the investor's **perception** — the manner in which they perceive risk, return, safety, liquidity, and the credibility of the instrument.

Among the various investment avenues available — such as bank deposits, mutual funds, shares, gold, real estate, and post office schemes — **life insurance** holds a distinct place. It is the only instrument that offers a **dual benefit**: it provides life cover (risk protection) while simultaneously serving as a medium of long-term savings and investment.

Life insurance is a contract between the policyholder and the insurer, in which the insurer promises to pay a designated sum of money (the sum assured) to the nominee upon the death of the insured person, or on the maturity of the policy, in exchange for a premium. This unique combination of **security and savings** influences how investors perceive it.

**Perception** refers to the process by which individuals select, organise, and interpret information to form a meaningful picture. In the context of life insurance, investor perception is shaped by factors such as:
- Awareness of insurance products
- Trust in the insurance company
- Returns and bonuses offered
- Tax benefits available
- Service quality and claim settlement experience
- Influence of agents, family, and advertisements

Understanding this perception is important because it directly affects the demand for insurance products, the design of new products, and the marketing strategies of insurance companies.

## 1.2 Meaning and Definition of Life Insurance

> "Life insurance is a contract whereby the insurer, in consideration of a premium paid either in a lump sum or in periodical instalments, undertakes to pay an annuity or a certain sum of money either on the death of the insured or on the expiry of a certain number of years."

In simple terms, life insurance is a tool that protects the financial future of a family in case of the unfortunate death of the breadwinner, and also helps in wealth creation through savings-linked plans.

## 1.3 Features of Life Insurance

1. **Contract of insurance** – A legally binding agreement between insurer and insured.
2. **Risk coverage** – Provides protection against the financial risk of death.
3. **Premium payment** – The policyholder pays a premium periodically or as a lump sum.
4. **Long-term commitment** – Most policies run for 10, 15, 20 years or more.
5. **Sum assured** – A guaranteed amount payable on death or maturity.
6. **Investment cum protection** – Combines savings and protection.
7. **Tax benefits** – Premiums and maturity proceeds enjoy tax exemptions under prevailing tax laws.

## 1.4 Types of Life Insurance Plans

| Type of Plan | Key Feature |
|---------------|-------------|
| **Term Insurance** | Pure protection plan; high cover at low premium; no maturity benefit |
| **Endowment Plan** | Protection + savings; pays sum assured on death or maturity |
| **Money-Back Plan** | Periodic payouts during the policy term + maturity benefit |
| **Whole Life Plan** | Cover for the entire life of the insured |
| **Unit Linked Insurance Plan (ULIP)** | Insurance + market-linked investment |
| **Pension / Retirement Plan** | Builds a corpus for regular income after retirement |
| **Child Plan** | Secures a child's education and future needs |

# CHAPTER 2: INDUSTRY & COMPANY PROFILE

## 2.1 Introduction to the Insurance Industry

Insurance is one of the oldest forms of risk management. The basic principle is the **pooling of risk** — a large number of people contribute small amounts (premiums) into a common fund, from which compensation is paid to those who suffer a loss. Insurance is broadly classified into:

1. **Life Insurance** – Covers the risk of death and provides savings/investment benefits.
2. **General (Non-Life) Insurance** – Covers risks related to health, motor, fire, marine, property, etc.

This study focuses on the **life insurance** segment.

## 2.2 History of Life Insurance in India

- **1818** – The Oriental Life Insurance Company was established in Kolkata, marking the beginning of life insurance business in India.
- **1912** – The Indian Life Assurance Companies Act was the first statute to regulate the life insurance business.
- **1956** – The Government of India nationalised the life insurance sector and established the **Life Insurance Corporation of India (LIC)** by merging around 245 insurance companies and provident societies.
- **1999** – The **Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority (IRDA)** Act was passed, opening up the sector to private and foreign players.
- **2000 onwards** – Private insurers such as ICICI Prudential, HDFC Life, SBI Life, Max Life, and others entered the market, increasing competition and product innovation.
- **Recent years** – Increased Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) limits, growth of digital insurance, and the listing of LIC as a public company have transformed the industry.

## 2.3 Structure of the Indian Life Insurance Industry

The industry today consists of:
- **One public sector insurer:** Life Insurance Corporation of India (LIC)
- **Several private sector insurers**, often in joint ventures with foreign partners.

The sector is regulated by the **Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India (IRDAI)**, which protects policyholders' interests, regulates premiums, ensures fair practices, and promotes the orderly growth of the industry.

## 2.4 Role of IRDAI

The **Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India (IRDAI)** is the apex body that:
- Issues licences to insurers and intermediaries (agents, brokers).
- Protects the interests of policyholders.
- Regulates investment of funds by insurance companies.
- Ensures financial soundness and solvency of insurers.
- Frames regulations for product approval, claims, and grievance redressal.

## 2.5 Importance of Life Insurance in the Economy

1. **Provides financial security** to families against uncertainties.
2. **Mobilises savings** and channels them into productive investment.
3. **Contributes to capital formation** and infrastructure development.
4. **Generates employment** through agents, advisors, and staff.
5. **Promotes the habit of long-term saving** among individuals.
6. **Supports the government** through investment in securities and bonds.

# CHAPTER 3: REVIEW OF LITERATURE

A review of literature helps in understanding the work already done in a particular field, identifying research gaps, and building a strong foundation for the present study. The following studies relate to investor perception and behaviour towards life insurance.

> **Note for the student:** The reviews below are summarised in a standard academic style. Please verify each reference and adjust the year/journal as per the sources you actually access through your college library or Google Scholar. Do not present a source you have not personally checked.

1. **Sharma & Sharma** – In their study on investor perception towards life insurance, the authors observed that **safety and security of the family** is the strongest motive for purchasing life insurance, while tax benefits and savings are secondary motives. They concluded that awareness programmes can significantly improve penetration.

2. **Gupta** – This study examined the factors influencing the purchase of life insurance policies and found that **trust in the company, brand image, and the role of agents** are key influencers, particularly for first-time buyers.

3. **Rao** – The author analysed customer satisfaction in the life insurance sector and reported that **claim settlement efficiency and after-sales service** are the most important determinants of satisfaction, even more than returns.

4. **Kumar & Priya** – Their comparative study between LIC and private insurers found that customers continue to **trust LIC** for its government backing and reliability, but **private insurers** are preferred by younger, urban investors for their innovative products and digital convenience.

5. **Singh** – This study on the awareness of life insurance products concluded that a large number of policyholders are **unaware of the full range of products** and often buy policies merely on the recommendation of agents or for tax-saving purposes.

6. **Joseph & Thomas** – The researchers studied the impact of demographic factors and found that **age, income, education, and occupation significantly influence** the type and amount of life insurance purchased.

7. **Verma** – The study highlighted that **ULIPs and market-linked products** are perceived as risky by conservative investors, who prefer traditional endowment and money-back plans for guaranteed returns.

8. **Mehta** – This research emphasised the growing role of **digital channels and bancassurance** in distribution and noted that ease of access and transparency increase investor confidence.

## Research Gap

1. **Increase awareness:** Since 48% are only moderately aware and 26% are unaware of products, insurers should conduct **awareness and financial literacy campaigns**, especially explaining the difference between protection (term) and savings (endowment/ULIP) plans.
2. **Educate on term insurance:** As protection is the main motive, companies should promote **affordable term plans** that offer high cover at low premiums.
3. **Improve transparency in returns:** Investors prioritise safety and returns; insurers should clearly communicate **expected returns, bonuses, and charges** to build trust.
4. **Strengthen digital channels:** With 16% relying on the internet/social media, insurers should invest in **user-friendly websites, apps, and online policy servicing**.
5. **Faster claim settlement:** Quick and hassle-free claim settlement greatly improves satisfaction and word-of-mouth recommendation.
6. **Train and support agents:** As agents are the top influencers, companies should ensure they are **well-trained and ethical** in advising customers.
7. **Target the uninsured 16%:** Focused campaigns can convert non-policyholders, particularly young earners, into customers.
8. **Customised products:** Develop products tailored to different income and age groups, including child and retirement plans.

## 6.3 Conclusion

The study on the perception of investors investing in life insurance reveals that life insurance is widely accepted as an important instrument of **financial security and savings**. The primary motive for investment is the **protection of the family**, followed by tax benefits and savings.

**LIC** continues to dominate investor preference owing to its trust and reliability, but **private insurers** are steadily gaining ground, especially among younger and urban investors. While the overall perception towards life insurance is **positive** — reflected in high satisfaction and recommendation levels — there exists a significant **awareness gap** regarding the variety of products available.

Insurers can strengthen investor confidence by focusing on **customer education, transparency, prompt service, and digital convenience**. With rising income levels, increasing financial awareness, and growing digital adoption, the life insurance sector in India holds strong potential for further growth.
